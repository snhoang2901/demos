RESEARCH FINDINGS - WHY THE PROPOSAL FORMAT EXISTS
Knowledge file for the "Ceco Proposal Builder" Copilot agent.

USE OF THIS FILE
- Use these findings only to explain why the proposal is structured the way it is.
- Repeat them as written. Do not add survey quotes, percentages, respondent counts or new findings.
- Always show the source label next to a finding.

SOURCE STATUS (read before presenting to executives)
The survey screenshots (image (4) to image (7)) could not be machine-read when these findings were written.
The findings below are therefore based on the sample Ceco Customer Proposal Report (CPT) dated 10/19/2026
and the myPortal walkthrough. Before using them in front of executives, the account team should compare
them with the survey screenshots and update the wording and source labels to match what the survey says.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 1 - Source: sample CPT
What we heard:    The sample proposal is 30 pages long, and the total first appears on page 19, after the
                  repeated building specifications.
Why it matters:   The customer has to dig for the number they care about most, and the builder ends up
                  walking them through the document.
What we improved: A short proposal with the total, delivery date and open decisions on page 1. Specs move
                  to an appendix.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 2 - Source: myPortal walkthrough
What we heard:    Builders need to add their own scope (foundations, windows, doors, builder services) on
                  top of the myPortal output, without changing it.
Why it matters:   Otherwise the customer sees only the steel package, or the builder rebuilds the proposal
                  by hand.
What we improved: A builder-branded proposal with a separate "[Builder] provides" column and priced builder
                  lines next to the unchanged Ceco total.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 3 - Source: sample CPT
What we heard:    What's excluded isn't stated. In the sample, Exclusions and the other financial sections
                  still say "Enter a description here".
Why it matters:   When exclusions aren't written down, the customer can assume items are included, and scope
                  questions surface later.
What we improved: Ceco scope, builder scope and a "Not included" list side by side, plus a responsibility
                  matrix.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 4 - Source: myPortal walkthrough + sample CPT
What we heard:    Outputs are only as good as the inputs. The sample has open items nobody has flagged: no
                  snow load shown, color TBD, credit terms TBD and conflicting coating specs.
Why it matters:   Gaps get found late, or by the customer.
What we improved: A builder review list of flagged items with owners, plus customer questions that are
                  ready to send.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
FINDING 5 - Source: sample CPT
What we heard:    The content is technical and repetitive. Loads and deflection limits repeat for each
                  building, alongside shorthand like "Wind Exposure: 107.0000 psf".
Why it matters:   The owner can't easily tell what they're buying, and turning it into customer language
                  takes the builder time.
What we improved: Plain-language notes with the original CPT wording and page alongside, and specs
                  consolidated once in the appendix.
